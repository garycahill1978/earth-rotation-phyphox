Measuring the Earth's Rotation with phyphox

Can an ordinary smartphone directly measure the rotation of the Earth?

Gary Cahill
South East Technological University, Ireland
The Earth rotates at only about 0.00418° per second.

The signal is extremely small and can be obscured by gyroscope noise, bias and drift.
A 180° reversal changes the sign of the Earth-rotation component, allowing it to be separated from the much larger sensor bias.

The ABBA reversal method

Measurements are made in one orientation, after a 180° reversal, and then again after returning to the original orientation.

A1 → B1 → B2 → A2
Δω = (A1 + A2) / 2 − (B1 + B2) / 2

Why ABBA?

A 180° reversal changes the sign of the Earth-rotation signal, while the gyroscope bias does not change.

The ABBA sequence is symmetric in time:

(tA1 + tA2) / 2  =  (tB1 + tB2) / 2

The average measurement time is the same for A and B, suppressing linear drift.

180° reversal cancels constant bias  •  ABBA timing suppresses linear drift
